TL;DR

Agima is one autoregressive omni model that is both policy and world model: it interleaves actions with imagined frames of where they lead, and plans from those frames before acting.

Imagination formats: linear rollout, counterfactual branching, forward thinking. Training: SFT on these formats, then RL with only a binary task-success reward.

01Method

How Agima imagines

Actions and imagined frames are generated in one sequence; each imagined frame is the observation for the next action.

Linear rollout

Imagine the frame after each action; plan on from it.

Counterfactual branching

Imagine an alternative action; reject it if it fails.

Forward thinking

Imagine complete candidate plans; commit to the one that succeeds.

04Planning traps

Moves that look fine, until they don’t

Test sets where a locally reasonable move fails several steps later. K = ∞.

Deadlock

A legal push leaves a box that can never reach a goal. 2, 3 and 4 boxes, 100 puzzles each.

Structural Fork

At a critical step several pushes are legal; only some keep the puzzle solvable. 2, 3 and 4 boxes, 100 puzzles each.

Pacman Route-order

The nearer food first gets Pacman caught; the farther food first clears the level. 8×8 grids with 2 to 8 food, plus 10×10 with 8.

05Agentic RL

Learning when and where to imagine

RL from the SFT models on three-box Sokoban, with only a binary success reward.

A

RL improves held-out planning

Every imagination variant improves, including on four-box puzzles unseen in RL; the action-only model stays near zero.

B

A second plan only when needed

Second plans drop from 79% to 26–31% of episodes, follow a predicted failure of the first, and use the unified format.

C

Branching moves to critical decisions

More branches per episode, placed more often where one push makes the puzzle unsolvable.
