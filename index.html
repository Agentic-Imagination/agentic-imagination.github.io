Agima (Agentic Imagination) trains an agent to imagine before it acts: a single omni model generates images of where its actions lead and plans over these imagined futures.

How Agima imagines

Agima generates actions and imagined frames in one sequence. Each imagined frame is the observation for the next action.

Environments

We train Agima on the easy split of four environments and test it on a harder split it never sees in training.

Easy-to-hard generalization

K is the number of actions Agima executes per real observation. Between observations, it plans from its own imagined frames.

Planning traps

Test sets where a locally reasonable move fails several steps later. K = ∞.

Deadlock

One push puts the nearer box on the goal beside it. That goal is the only one the farther box can ever reach, so the farther box is left with nowhere to go.

A 2-box level from Agima's Sokoban generator. Test sets: 2, 3 and 4 boxes, 100 puzzles each.

Irreversible push (Structural Fork set)

Two pushes are legal here. Pushing up pins a box to the wall in a row with no goal, and no later move brings it back. Pushing right keeps the puzzle solvable.

Test sets: 2, 3 and 4 boxes, 100 puzzles each.

Pacman route order

Going for the nearer food first leaves Pacman no escape from the ghost. Going for the farther food first clears the level.

The episode from Figure 3 of the paper. Test grids are 8×8 with 2, 4, 6 or 8 food, and 10×10 with 8.

Learning when and where to imagine

We run RL from the SFT models on three-box Sokoban, with a binary success reward and nothing else.

RL improves held-out planning

Every imagination variant improves, including on four-box puzzles it never sees in RL, while the action-only model stays near zero.

A new unified format emerges

After RL, Agima rolls out one plan step by step and drafts the next only once it foresees a failure. Second plans drop from 79% to 26–31% of episodes.

Branching moves to critical decisions

After RL, Agima branches more often, and more of its branches land where one push would make the puzzle unsolvable.
